Market Calendar

Plumbing Around High Point Market

High Point Market happens in April and October, when the downtown showroom district fills with visitors across roughly 11.5 million square feet of space. Restrooms, water heaters and drains are under heavy use for several days. A failed toilet or a slow drain in a showroom building in Market week is far more disruptive than at any other time.

Property managers and tenants do best with a walkthrough a few weeks before each Market. Check restroom fixtures, shutoff valves, water heater temperature and drain flow, and schedule repairs in the quiet weeks. This is routine, inexpensive maintenance.

Code And Compliance

Fixtures, Grease And Backflow In Commercial Buildings

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the NC Plumbing Code follows, sets minimum fixture counts by occupancy, so a showroom that adds seating or a restaurant that expands needs the restrooms to keep up. Restaurants generally need a grease interceptor or trap, and its upkeep matters. Backflow assemblies on commercial lines are usually tested annually, as described under backflow testing and prevention.

Hot water demand is another commercial issue. Kitchens, salons and hotels need heaters sized for peak use, not average use. A sizing review before a replacement avoids an undersized unit that runs out during a rush.
