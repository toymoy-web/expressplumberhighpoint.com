What It Is

How Hydro Jetting Cleans A Pipe

A hydro jetter pushes water through a hose and a nozzle at pressures commonly in the thousands of pounds per square inch. Nozzles fire jets backward to pull the hose along the pipe and forward to break up blockages. The water scours the whole circumference of the pipe, not only a hole through the middle of a clog.

That difference matters. A cable can open a line quickly, but it leaves grease, scale and root hair clinging to the walls. The next clog forms where the last one was left. Jetting removes the coating, which is why it is often the answer for lines that clog every few months.

When It Helps

Problems Hydro Jetting Solves

Grease buildup

Kitchen lines slowly narrow as fat cools and coats the pipe wall.

Root hair

Fine roots in clay or cast-iron joints catch paper and grow back.

Mineral and rust scale

Cast-iron lines develop a rough crust that snags debris.

Sludge and soap film

Washing machine and shower lines coat over time.

Recurring main line clogs

Cabling clears it, then it returns.

Slow commercial drains

Restaurant and kitchen lines see heavy grease loads.

Honest Limits

When Not To Jet

Jetting is not for every pipe. A collapsed line, a badly offset joint or fragile old materials can be damaged by high pressure. That is why a camera runs first. The video shows whether the pipe is sound enough, and whether jetting will help or only move the problem.

Scale can also hide cracks. In old cast iron, removing the crust sometimes reveals leaks the scale was sealing. The plumber should say so before the job. If the camera shows a broken pipe, professional sewer line repair is the real fix and jetting just prepares the line.

Prepare

What To Do Before Jetting Day

A little preparation makes the visit faster and keeps your property clean.

  1. 1

    Find the cleanout, a capped pipe in the yard, crawlspace or basement. The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the NC code follows, expects accessible cleanouts in drainage systems.

  2. 2

    Clear a path and move vehicles, planters and stored items away from the work area.

  3. 3

    Tell the plumber about past clogs, earlier repairs and any known root problems.

  4. 4

    Do not run water during the visit unless asked to, since the line may be open.

  5. 5

    Keep children and pets away from hoses and the work area, because jet hoses are under high pressure.

Local Fit

Where Jetting Pays Off In The High Point Area

Older neighborhoods in the Uptown Suburbs district still carry cast-iron stacks and clay laterals, and mature trees line streets such as those in Emerywood. Those conditions produce the exact mix of scale and root hair that jetting handles well. Mill houses near Highland Cotton Mills often have small branch lines coated with decades of film.

A typical home sewer lateral is four inches across. The plumbing code expects drain pipe that size to slope at least one-eighth of an inch per foot, so waste keeps moving. A sag in the line holds water, and grease and roots settle there first.

Commercial kitchens, such as downtown restaurants that fill up when Market visitors are in town, generate more grease than a residential line is built for. They usually need scheduled jetting alongside a working grease trap. If you run a business, ask about commercial plumbing service service as well.

Comparison

Jetting Versus Chemical Drain Openers

Chemical openers

  • Can sit in a standing line and soften old joints
  • Leave a hazard for the plumber
  • Rarely clear grease or roots
  • Do nothing for scale

Hydro jetting

  • Uses only water at controlled pressure
  • Scours the full pipe wall
  • Works on grease, scale and root hair
  • Paired with a camera for proof

After The Jetting

Keeping A Jetted Line Clear

A line that has just been jetted starts fresh, but habits decide how long it stays that way. Grease poured down a kitchen drain rebuilds a coating, and wipes sold as flushable do not break down like toilet paper. A camera view after jetting shows whether the pipe is smooth or still scarred.

Roots are the usual reason a line fills again. Jetting cuts them back but does not kill the tree that feeds them. A line with repeat root growth is often put on a camera and jetting schedule matched to how fast the roots return, and drain cleaning covers the lighter jobs in between.
