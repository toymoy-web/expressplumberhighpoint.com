How It Works

What A Sump Pump Does And Where It Lives

A sump pump sits in a pit, called a sump basin, at the lowest point of a basement or crawlspace. Groundwater collects in the pit, a float switch rises, and the pump sends water out through a discharge pipe away from the foundation. Without it, that water goes into the structure instead.

Many High Point-area houses have crawlspaces instead of basements, and a pit in a crawlspace is just as useful. The Piedmont's clay-heavy soils drain slowly, so after a long rain the water table can sit close to the floor for days.

Emergency Sump Failure

Emergency Sump Pump Failure During A Storm

A sump pump that stops during heavy rain is an emergency because the pit can overflow in under an hour. If the pump hums without moving water or the float is stuck, lift the float by hand to see if it starts. Do not stand in water near electrical outlets.

Call the emergency line if the pit is full, the power is out or the basement is already wet. A plumber can swap a failed pump or float and test the discharge line. Ask about a battery backup so the next outage is covered.

Failure Signs

Signs Your Sump Pump Is Failing

Pump runs constantly

A stuck float or an overwhelmed pump never turns off.

Pump does not start

A dead motor, tripped breaker or jammed float.

Strange noises

Grinding or rattling often means a worn impeller or debris.

Visible rust or age

Many pumps last around 7 to 10 years.

Water in the pit won't drop

The pump runs but moves little water.

Musty smell or damp insulation

Water is staying in the crawlspace longer than it should.

Before The Storm

What To Do Before Heavy Rain

A few minutes of testing before a forecast storm can prevent a crawlspace flood.

  1. 1

    Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and confirm the float rises and the pump starts and empties it.

  2. 2

    Check that the discharge pipe is clear and carries water at least several feet away from the foundation.

  3. 3

    Make sure the pump is on its own outlet and that the outlet works.

  4. 4

    Test the battery backup by unplugging the primary pump and watching the backup run.

  5. 5

    Remove debris from the pit and keep the lid sealed to stop gases and moisture.

  6. 6

    Look at gutters and downspouts, since water from the roof often feeds the pit.

Discharge Rules

Where The Water Goes Matters

Installation follows the NC Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code, along with the pump manufacturer's installation guidelines. Discharge should run well away from the house, onto ground that slopes away. Many municipalities prohibit connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer, because the extra flow can overload the system. Ask the plumber to confirm local rules for your address before choosing a discharge route.

A check valve on the discharge pipe stops water from flowing back into the pit after each cycle. If your pump short-cycles, a failed check valve is a frequent cause. The same problem can make the pump work twice as hard and fail early.

Local Conditions

Why Sump Systems Matter In This Area

High Point sits in the rolling Piedmont, where the headwaters of the Deep River collect in the city's Oak Hollow Lake and City Lake watershed. Heavy rain, including the remnants of tropical systems, can saturate clay soils and raise the water table under older houses. Mill houses and Uptown Suburbs homes with shallow crawlspaces take the first hit.

A wet crawlspace feeds mold, rots subfloors and damages insulation. If water has already come in, water damage restoration service handles the drying, and leak detection service can rule out a plumbing source. Homes in Trinity and other southern Randolph County areas, where soil is similar, call about the same issue.

Repair Or Replace

When A Sump Pump Repair Makes Sense

A repair makes sense when the pump is under about seven years old and the fault is a single part, such as a float switch or check valve. Those repairs are quick and cost far less than a new unit. If the pump has cycled hard through several wet seasons, the motor often fails soon after a repair, and replacement is the better use of money.

Ask for both prices before work begins. A good estimate names the failed part, the labor, and what a full replacement would cost, so you can decide with the numbers in front of you. If the pit is full and rain is coming, a temporary pump or a quick replacement may be the safer call.

Ejector Pumps

Sewage Ejector Pumps Are A Different Machine

A sewage ejector pump lifts waste from a basement bathroom up to the sewer line, and it is built differently from a sump pump. A failed ejector is more urgent because it backs up a toilet or shower. Tell the dispatcher which pump you have. A sealed lid, a check valve and a vent are part of an ejector setup, and the plumber checks each when replacing the pump.
