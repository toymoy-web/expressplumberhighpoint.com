Why It Matters

Septic Tank Pumping For High Point-Area Homes

About half of the homes in North Carolina use septic systems, according to the state's public health department. In the High Point area, that includes many properties outside municipal sewer lines in Guilford, Randolph and Davidson counties.

The EPA says the average American uses about 82 gallons of water at home each day, and a septic system has to treat all of it. A septic tank holds solids while liquid flows out to the drainfield, and the solids need to be pumped out before they reach the outlet.

State guidance says most tanks should be pumped every three to five years, and Guilford County Environmental Health gives the same range. A tank that is never pumped can overflow or clog the drainfield, and drainfield repair costs far more than a pump-out. Pumping is the least expensive part of owning a septic system.

Septic Emergencies

Emergency Septic Backup: What To Do First

A septic system is in emergency territory when sewage rises in the lowest drains, several fixtures back up together or the yard smells strongly of sewage. Stop using water, including laundry and dishwashers, because every gallon adds to the problem. Then call the emergency line.

Tell the dispatcher when the tank was last pumped and whether you know where the lid is. A full tank is the easy answer, but a blocked outlet baffle, a failing drain field or a pump problem can look the same from inside the house.

Warning Signs

Signs Your Septic Tank Needs Attention

Slow drains in every fixture

When the whole house slows at once, the tank or its outlet is often full.

Gurgling pipes

Air trapped by a blocked outlet bubbles back through toilets and drains.

Sewage smell

Odors indoors or around the tank lid point to a leak or a full tank.

Soggy spots in the yard

Wet, smelly ground over the drainfield means effluent is surfacing.

Alarm on the pump tank panel

A red light or beeping on a pump system signals a high-water or failed pump condition.

Lush patch of grass

A bright green strip over the drainfield can mean it is getting too much effluent.

Regulation

Who Is Allowed To Pump And Haul Septic Waste

North Carolina rules require pumping and hauling of septic waste to be done by a septage management firm permitted by the state under G.S. 130A-291.1. Whoever you hire should be able to name their permit and the approved facility where the waste goes.

Routine pumping does not need a permit from you. Repairs, tank replacement or a bedroom addition are different, because they need approval from the county's environmental health office. In Guilford County, that office issues the permits and keeps the septic records for each property.

Safety

Septic Tank Safety: What Not To Do

Septic tanks produce gases that can harm or kill, so the tank is not a place to look into or climb into.

  1. 1

    Never lean into, enter or lower anything into an open septic tank. Gases such as hydrogen sulfide and methane build up inside.

  2. 2

    Keep the lids closed and secured, especially where children play, and replace cracked or rotted lids right away.

  3. 3

    Keep pets and people away from wet, surfacing sewage in the yard until the cause is fixed.

  4. 4

    Do not light flames or smoke near an open tank or a strong sewer-gas smell.

  5. 5

    Leave pumping, inspection and repairs to people trained and permitted to do them.

  3. 3

  4. 4

    You get the tank condition and a suggested next pump date.

Keep It Working

Habits That Protect A Septic System

Guilford County Environmental Health and the state health department give similar advice.

  1. 1

    Pump the tank every three to five years, and ask that both sides of the tank be pumped.

  2. 2

    Keep grease, chemicals and cleaning solutions out of the drains.

  3. 3

    Limit use of the garbage disposal, which adds solids.

  4. 4

    Never send water softener backwash into the septic tank.

  5. 5

    Spread laundry across the week instead of running many loads in a day.

  6. 6

    Know where the tank and drainfield are, and keep cars off them.

Soft Water Bonus

High Point's Soft Water Helps Septic Owners

Because the city's water is soft, with hardness in the published reports running roughly 30 to 60 mg/L, most septic homes in the area have no reason to install a salt-based softener. That removes a common risk, since softener backwash is one of the things county health officials say to keep out of the tank.

Clay-heavy Piedmont soil also drains slowly, which is why drainfield health matters here. Local history in Archdale even notes that heavy clay was one reason residents wanted public water and sewer. If your yard stays wet after rain, tell the pumper so they can look at the drainfield and not only the tank.

Related Work

When It Is Not The Tank

If the tank is fine but the house line is clogged, drain cleaning and unclogging solves it. A broken pipe between house and tank is a job for emergency sewer line repair. Homeowners in Trinity and the surrounding Randolph County area often have septic, so that town page covers local details.

Access And Records

Find The Lid And Keep A Pumping Record

Locate the tank lid before pump day. A riser brought up to ground level makes pumping faster and easier to repeat than digging each time. The county septic file for the property holds the original permit and layout, which helps locate the tank and the drainfield.

Keep a record of each pumping date, the tank size and what the pumper found. Buyers often ask for that history, and it shows whether the interval you follow suits your household.

Around The Triad

Septic Country Around High Point

Septic tanks serve many homes outside town sewer lines in Trinity, Archdale, Randleman, Level Cross and New Salem. A house near the Deep River or Randleman Lake with a high water table will drain more slowly, so the tank and drainfield need more attention there. Heavy red clay in Archdale also slows drainfields.

Inside High Point, the older Uptown Suburbs and the Highland Cotton Mills Village are on city sewer, so a backup there points to the line, not a tank. Knowing which system serves your address is the first question the dispatcher asks.

County Records

Septic Records And Repairs In Guilford And Randolph County

Guilford County Environmental Health states that it has uploaded its system records to its GIS platform, where you can search an address and choose the Well and Septic option. The county also lists repair permits and construction authorizations among its septic services. Randolph County's septic office is in Asheboro.

Randolph County also states that a failing septic system must be repaired, with the owner given thirty days after a written notice to finish. If a backup has you worried about a notice, ask the county office what applies to your address before you pay for work.
