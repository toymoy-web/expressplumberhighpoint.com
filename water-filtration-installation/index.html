Start With The Report

Read The City's Water Quality Report First

The City of High Point publishes an annual drinking water quality report, listing the water sources, treatment steps and test results. High Point draws its water from Oak Hollow Lake and Arnold Koonce City Lake and treats it at the Ward Water Plant. The report tells you what is in the water that arrives at the meter.

A good plumber starts there and asks what you notice at the tap. Chlorine taste, sediment, rust or a smell from the heater each point to a different solution. Water that is safe by the report can still taste poor, and that is a legitimate reason to filter.

Match The Filter

Filter Types And What They Do

Filters are certified against specific NSF/ANSI standards. The standard tells you what a product has been tested to reduce.

What You Notice

Why Does My Water Taste, Smell Or Look Wrong?

Pool-like chlorine taste

Disinfectant residue. A carbon filter reduces it.

Rust-colored water on first use

Often old galvanized lines or a corroding water heater, not the supply.

Cloudy or gritty water

Sediment from the main or from scale inside old pipes.

Rotten egg smell in hot water only

Usually a reaction in the water heater tank, fixed by a flush and a new anode rod.

Metallic taste

Metals from corroding fixtures or pipe. Worth testing.

Smell in both hot and cold water

Points to the supply or a well, so test the source.

Older Houses

Lead, Old Solder And Old Fixtures

Lead was banned in plumbing solder and in new brass fixtures in 1986. Since January 2014, federal law defines lead-free plumbing as a weighted average of no more than 0.25 percent lead on wetted surfaces. A house built before 1986 may therefore have lead solder in copper joints or lead in older fixtures.

The Uptown Suburbs district and mill houses fall into this age range, and plumbers also meet galvanized lines that shed rust. Flush a tap for a minute after the water has sat overnight, and consider testing.

If lead is a worry, a filter certified for lead reduction under NSF/ANSI 53 at the kitchen tap is a practical step. The long-term fix is removing the lead sources, which may involve whole-home repiping.

Placement

Whole-House Versus Under-Sink

Whole-house system

  • Treats all the water entering the home
  • Protects showers and appliances from chlorine and sediment
  • Needs space and a pressure-rated installation
  • Higher up-front cost

Under-sink or point-of-use

  • Filters drinking and cooking water only
  • Easy to certify for health contaminants
  • Smaller footprint and cost
  • Does not treat shower or laundry water
Preparing

What To Do Before A Filtration Visit

A little information makes the recommendation more accurate.

  1. 1

    Note what you notice: chlorine smell, rust stains, cloudy water, a metallic taste or a rotten egg smell.

  2. 2

    Find the city's latest annual water quality report, or tell the plumber if you are on a well.

  3. 3

    Tell the plumber if anyone in the house is pregnant, has an infant or has a weakened immune system, since those cases call for certified filters.

  4. 4

    Know where the main shutoff valve is.

  5. 5

    Say how many people live in the house, which affects filter sizing.

Soft Water

Why A Filter, Not A Softener, Fits City Water

The city's water is soft, so a softener on city water mostly removes hardness that is not there. A carbon filter fixes the complaints people actually have, taste and odor, without salt or a drain line. If your complaint is hard water, check first if you are on a private well, and see water softener installation and repair.

Filters need new cartridges on a schedule. An unchanged carbon cartridge can become a place where bacteria grow, so set a reminder and keep the receipt for the cartridge model.
