Safety First

If You Smell Gas, Leave Before You Call Anyone

Natural gas has a sulfur-like odor added so leaks can be noticed. If you smell it, get everyone and every pet out of the house, then call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not flip light switches, use the phone inside, start the car in an attached garage or relight a pilot.

A plumber should come after the utility has made the situation safe. This page covers the planned side of gas piping: adding lines, replacing old ones and repairing known problems. For a gas water heater that is acting up, see water heater repair service.

Sizing And Code

How Gas Lines Are Sized And Inspected

Most homes receive gas at about seven inches of water column, a little over a quarter pound per square inch. Gas piping is sized from the total BTU load of all appliances, the length of the run and the pipe material.

Too small a line starves appliances at high demand, which is why tankless heaters and big ranges often expose old, undersized piping. In North Carolina, gas piping and appliance venting fall under the NC Fuel Gas Code, which is built on the national NFPA 54 standard.

Gas work normally needs a permit and an inspection with a pressure test. The contractor installing gas piping should hold the right North Carolina license, and you can ask which license covers the work. The NC State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors is the licensing body.

Materials

Black Iron Pipe Versus CSST

Black iron pipe

  • Rigid, long-lived steel pipe
  • Traditional for rough-in piping
  • More fittings and labor
  • Resists damage well

CSST (flexible stainless tubing)

  • Flexible, fast to run through walls
  • Fewer fittings
  • Must be bonded as the manufacturer requires
  • Needs protection where it passes through framing
Local Context

Gas In Older High Point Homes

Many 1940s to 1980s ranches and mill houses were piped for a stove and a furnace, and sometimes a water heater. Adding a tankless heater, a gas fireplace or a generator to that old piping can overload it. Rusty black iron in a crawlspace should be checked for corrosion, and old flexible connectors behind a range are often worth replacing.

Propane houses outside the gas utility's mains, common in rural pockets around the Piedmont, use tanks and regulators instead of a meter. Piping from the tank follows similar rules, though pressures differ. A carbon monoxide alarm near the bedrooms is wise in any house with fuel-burning appliances. For a new tankless unit, also read about tankless water heater maintenance.
