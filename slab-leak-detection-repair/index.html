What Is A Slab Leak

Water Lines Under A Concrete Foundation

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe that runs in or under the concrete foundation of a house. A leak of just one gallon a minute wastes 1,440 gallons a day. Many 1940s to 1980s homes on the south side of High Point were built this way, with supply lines, and often drains, buried below the floor. The pipe cannot be seen or reached without breaking concrete, so the leak can run a long time before anyone notices.

That is why locating it precisely matters. Guessing at a spot can mean a hole in the wrong place. A technician confirms the leak with a meter test, then uses acoustic and thermal tools to mark it. The page on professional leak detection describes those methods in more detail.

Symptoms

Signs Of A Slab Leak

Warm spot on the floor

A hot-water line leaking warms the concrete above it.

Sound of running water

A hiss with every fixture closed.

Meter moves with water off

Confirms water is escaping somewhere.

Wet carpet or buckled flooring

Moisture rising through the slab.

Cracks in walls or floors

Water can erode soil under the slab over time.

High bill or low pressure

Leaks lose water before it reaches the tap.

Why They Happen

What Causes Slab Leaks In This Area

Copper lines can wear against concrete and gravel as they expand and contract with hot water. Corrosion, high water pressure and poor original installation add to the stress. The Piedmont's red clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, and slow foundation movement can flex pipes over the years.

Hot-water lines tend to fail first because heat speeds wear. In older homes, galvanized lines under the slab can corrode through. Homes built roughly 1978 to 1995 may also have polybutylene tubing, which has a known failure history. Any of these can start the leak.

Honest Trade-offs

Spot Repair Versus Reroute

Spot repair

  • Lower cost for a single leak
  • Concrete is cut and repaired in one area
  • Leaves other old pipe in place
  • May need repeating if more leaks follow

Overhead reroute

  • Avoids breaking more slab
  • Replaces aging pipe with new lines
  • Usually higher up-front cost
  • Often ends the repair cycle
If You Suspect One

What To Do If You Think You Have A Slab Leak

Slab leaks are rarely dangerous at first, but they get worse with time. Plumbing work in North Carolina follows the NC Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

  1. 1

    Do the meter test: shut off all water use and see whether the meter still moves.

  2. 2

    Close the main valve if you see water on the floor or hear it running, and call.

  3. 3

    Feel floors for warm spots, especially in the kitchen, bathrooms and hallways.

  4. 4

    Stay away from any wet floor near outlets or appliances.

  5. 5

    Do not break the slab yourself, since cutting into a pressurized line can make the leak worse.

  6. 6

    Photograph wet flooring and damage for your insurance claim.

After The Leak

Water, Insurance And Flooring

Water under a slab can wet carpet, pad, baseboards and walls, and long-running leaks can encourage mold. If floors are already soaked, water damage cleanup and drying handles the drying. Some homeowners policies cover the access needed to reach a covered leak, while others exclude it, so read your policy and ask your insurer before work starts.

Keep the leak report, photos and invoices. They support a claim and also help future buyers understand what was done. Slab leak help is available to homes in Randleman and the other nearby towns as well.

While You Wait

Protecting Floors And Belongings

If you suspect a slab leak, close the main water valve and check the meter to confirm. Lift rugs, move furniture off warm or damp floors and run a fan if the room feels humid.

Warm spots on a floor, a jump in the hot water bill or the sound of running water point to a hot line under the slab. Note when you first noticed each sign and what the meter reading was, since the details help the plumber narrow the search.

Choosing A Repair

Questions To Ask Before You Approve The Work

Ask whether the quote is for a spot repair or a reroute, which flooring is disturbed and who patches the concrete. Ask whether the line will be pressure tested after the work and whether a camera or tracer confirmed the leak location before anything was cut.

A second leak can show up on an older system within a few years of the first. The age and material of the pipe make a reroute decision easier, so ask the plumber to note both in the estimate.

Local Foundations

Slab Foundations Around High Point And Greensboro

Slab-on-grade houses are common in the subdivisions of Archdale, Jamestown and west Greensboro, where homes from the 1970s and 1980s often have copper lines under the concrete. Older neighborhoods such as the Uptown Suburbs more often have crawlspaces, so a hot spot on the floor is more likely to mean a slab leak in newer construction.

Heavy red clay in the Archdale area moves with moisture, and a moving foundation can stress buried lines. A leak found early is cheaper to fix, which is why a running meter with all fixtures off should be checked quickly.
