Quick Checks

What To Do Before Calling About No Hot Water

Two minutes of checking sometimes fixes the problem, and it always gives the plumber better information.

  1. 1

    On an electric heater, look at the breaker for the heater. If it tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call.

  2. 2

    Press the red reset button on the upper thermostat panel of an electric unit if the breaker is fine. That button trips when the water overheats.

  3. 3

    On a gas heater, check that the gas valve on the unit is set to on, and look at the pilot window if your model has one.

  4. 4

    Feel the floor around the tank. Water at the base changes the question from a repair to a leak.

  5. 5

    Check the temperature dial. A setting knocked down to the lowest mark is a common reason for lukewarm water.

  6. 6

    If you smell gas at any point, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility. Do not relight anything.

Decision Guide

Repair It Or Replace It

Repair makes sense when

  • The tank is not leaking from its body
  • The unit is under about 10 years old
  • The failed part is an element, thermostat, igniter or valve
  • Past repairs have been rare

Replace instead when

  • Water is seeping from the tank shell
  • The unit is past 12 to 15 years old
  • Rust shows at the bottom of the tank
  • Repairs are repeating every year or two
Gas Safety

Gas Water Heaters Need Extra Care

A gas water heater burns fuel inside your house and vents the exhaust through a flue. If the flue is blocked, disconnected or drafting backward, carbon monoxide can enter the living space. A working carbon monoxide detector near the bedrooms is the best protection, and a technician should check the vent pipe any time a gas heater is repaired.

In North Carolina, gas water heaters, their piping and their venting fall under the NC Fuel Gas Code, which is built on the national NFPA 54 fuel gas standard. Gas leaks and pilot problems are separate matters from a failed thermostat. If you smell gas, evacuate and call 911 or the gas utility before calling a plumber. Persistent gas supply faults often lead to gas line repair.

Common Parts

The Parts That Fail Most Often

Most repairs come down to a short list of parts. Electric tanks usually need a heating element or a thermostat. Gas tanks usually need a thermocouple or flame sensor, a gas control valve or an igniter. Any tank can also need a new temperature and pressure relief valve or a drain valve.

The plumber identifies the part from the model number on the rating plate, so have it ready when you call. A new element or thermostat is a small repair on a healthy tank. A leak from the tank body is different, because a failed steel tank cannot be patched and the answer is replacement.
