What Service Covers

What A Tankless Service Visit Includes

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap opens, which means its heat exchanger sees every gallon you use. Service focuses on keeping that exchanger clean and the sensors honest.

Many units need a flow of about half a gallon per minute to fire, depending on the model, so very low flow can look like a fault. A typical visit flushes the unit with a descaling solution through the isolation valves, cleans the inlet water filter screen and reads the error history stored in the controller.

On gas units the plumber also checks the vent, the burner, the flame sensor and the condensate drain on condensing models. Combustion and gas pressure matter because an undersized or dirty gas supply can mimic a failing heater. The visit ends with a flow and temperature test at several fixtures.

Soft Water

Does Soft Water Mean You Can Skip The Flush?

High Point's water is soft by national standards, with hardness in the city's published reports running roughly 30 to 60 mg/L. Scale accumulates much more slowly in that water than in the hard-water cities most tankless advice is written for. Even so, manufacturers commonly recommend an annual flush, and warranty paperwork may ask for proof of it.

A reasonable plan is to flush yearly, then read what comes out. If the flush water runs clear and the screen is clean, a longer interval may be fine in a light-use home. The technician should show you what was in the screen and the flush bucket rather than just reporting that it was done.

Decision Guide

Flush It, Repair It Or Replace It

Service or repair makes sense when

  • The unit is under about 15 years old
  • The fault is a sensor, igniter, screen or scale
  • Parts for the model are still available
  • Water pressure and gas supply test normal

Replacement makes sense when

  • The heat exchanger leaks
  • Parts are discontinued for the model
  • Repairs are repeating within a year
  • The unit was undersized for the household from the start
Honest Limits

What Service Cannot Fix

Some problems sit outside the heater. A unit installed without isolation valves cannot be flushed until valves are added, which is a small plumbing job but a real one. A gas line that is too small for the unit's maximum input will starve the burner at high flow, and no amount of cleaning cures that. In that case, gas line installation and repair is the actual solution.

Parts availability varies by brand and age. If a controller board for a discontinued model is back-ordered, the better answer may be a new unit, and the plumber should say so plainly. For that decision, see water heater replacement.

Fit For Your Home

Is Tankless A Good Match For Older High Point Houses?

Often yes, with conditions. Mill houses and 1940s ranches tend to have small gas lines sized for a tank and a stove, not a unit that can draw close to 200,000 BTU per hour. Venting also needs a path through an exterior wall or roof, which can be tricky in a crawlspace or a tight utility closet. Newer homes in the wooded subdivisions near Oak Hollow Lake are usually easier.

If the home is on a septic system outside the city sewer lines, remember that any softener backwash should stay out of the tank. That is another reason a tankless unit paired with plain soft city water is simple to run.

Gas, Venting And Drain

The Parts Owners Overlook: Gas Supply, Vent And Condensate

Most tankless trouble that looks like a heater problem starts around the heater. A gas unit needs a gas line sized for its full burner load, a vent that is tight and correctly sloped, and a clear path for exhaust. A condensing model also drips acidic condensate that must reach a drain, often through a neutralizer.

A service visit checks each of those. The plumber looks at the vent joints for gaps, confirms the condensate line is not blocked, and watches the unit fire at full demand to see that gas pressure holds. A model that shuts down only when two fixtures run often points to supply, not to the heater itself.

Good Records

What To Write Down After Every Flush

Keep the model number, the serial number and the date of each flush on a label near the unit or in a phone note. Manufacturers often tie warranty terms to documented maintenance, so a short record can settle a question years later.

Also note any error code that appears and what the unit was doing at the time. A code that shows once means little, while the same code every winter morning tells a plumber where to look.

Local Homes

Tankless Units In Older And Newer High Point Homes

Owners in the Uptown Suburbs and nearby neighborhoods who convert to tankless often want to free up space in a small utility room. Those older houses may need a larger gas line and new venting. Homes built in the 1970s and 1980s in west Greensboro and Jamestown often need the same work.

Newer homes in Archdale and Randleman usually have easier access to gas and venting, so service there is mostly flushing and error code diagnosis. Scale from city water builds on the heat exchanger over time, so an annual flush matters everywhere.

Brands And Hybrids

Hybrid Heat Pump Units And Other Models Owners Ask About

Owners often call after another company says it will replace a tankless unit but does not service it. Others own a hybrid heat pump heater and are unsure who can maintain it. Ask the dispatcher for the brand and model before booking so the visit matches the equipment.

A flush and service is a different job from a replacement, and some callers only want the flush. Say so at the start so the quote does not include a new unit you did not ask for.
