The Basics

What Backflow Is And Why It Is Regulated

Backflow means water flows backward in a pipe. Water from an irrigation system, boiler or commercial process can then be pulled into the drinking water supply. A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops this. A cross-connection is any link between drinking water and a possible contaminant.

North Carolina regulates cross-connection control in Title 15A, Subchapter 18C of its administrative code. Local water utilities run the programs. They set test schedules and keep records. The City of High Point's Public Services department can tell you what applies to your address.

Testing Rules

Who Can Test And How Often

A certified backflow assembly tester must do the testing, and the report goes to the utility. Commercial assemblies are usually tested every year. After North Carolina Senate Bill 166 in 2024, some utilities moved eligible residential irrigation systems to testing every three years.

Requirements vary by utility, so confirm yours. Some NC ordinances, such as the Town of Warrenton's, also require testers to recertify every two years and file reports within ten business days.

Utilities generally accept assemblies approved by the University of Southern California Foundation for Cross-Connection Control and Hydraulic Research. Reduced pressure zone assemblies are built to ASSE Standard 1013. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) sets where assemblies are required.

A failed assembly must be repaired or replaced by the utility's deadline. Sprinklers run from an outdoor spigot are generally not compliant with the NC Plumbing Code and can be flagged.

Preparing

What To Do Before A Backflow Test

A little preparation makes the visit short.

  1. 1

    Find the assembly. It is usually near the water meter, along the irrigation line or in a mechanical room, and may sit in a green box.

  2. 2

    Clear brush, mulch and debris around it so the tester can reach the test cocks and valves.

  3. 3

    Gather the test notice, the last report and any account number the utility gave you.

  4. 4

    Expect a short water shutoff while valves are closed for testing.

  5. 5

    Ask for a copy of the test report once it is filed.

The Visit

How A Backflow Test Goes

  1. 1

    Check in

    The tester confirms the assembly, location and account.

  2. 2

    Test

    Pressure readings are taken across the check valves and relief valve.

  3. 3

    Repair if needed

    Worn rubber parts and springs are replaced, then the assembly is retested.

  4. 4

    Report

    The result is submitted to the utility and a copy is provided.

Warning Signs

Signs An Assembly Needs Attention

Water discharging from an RPZ relief port

Often a sign the assembly is failing or debris is lodged in a check.

Leaks at test cocks or unions

Worn seals or loose fittings.

Frost damage

Cracked bodies after freeze exposure.

Tampering or removal

Missing assemblies create unprotected connections.

Past-due test notice

The utility can shut off service for non-compliance.

Age

Rubber parts wear out after years of service.

Local Notes

Who Needs Backflow Protection Around High Point

Restaurants, apartment buildings and showroom buildings across downtown often have domestic or fire-line assemblies that need annual testing. Homeowners with lawn sprinklers, especially those in newer subdivisions around Oak Hollow Lake, are the usual residential customers. Landlords and property managers should keep reports on file for each building.

Freezes are mild here but not absent, and above-ground assemblies can crack in a hard cold snap. Covering or draining the assembly before a forecast freeze costs little. For businesses, commercial plumbing work covers the wider work of keeping a building compliant.

Annual Tests

Annual Tests, Multiple Units And Replacements

Most backflow calls are about a yearly test notice, often for an irrigation or commercial assembly. Some owners have several assemblies on one property, and some need an old one removed and replaced. Give the dispatcher the number of assemblies, their sizes and the due date on the notice.

A test is a short visit when the assembly is in good shape. A failed assembly is repaired or replaced and retested, and the paperwork goes to the utility that sent the notice.
