What The Sewer Line Is And Who Owns It

The sewer line, or lateral, is the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the municipal sewer main under the street, or to a septic tank. Property owners are generally responsible for the private lateral on their side of the connection, while the city handles the main. Confirm the boundary with the City of High Point Public Services before assuming a repair is yours or theirs.

If your house uses a septic tank instead, the line from the house to the tank is still yours, and a clog in a septic home can have a different cause. In that case, septic tank pump-out is part of the diagnosis.

Emergency Sewer Repair

When A Sewer Problem Is An Emergency

A sewer problem is an emergency when sewage is coming up in a tub, shower or floor drain, or when several fixtures drain badly at once. Stop running water, keep children and pets away and call the emergency line. The first goal is to clear the backup and protect the floors.

After that, a camera shows what failed, whether that is roots, a crack, a belly or a collapse. The repair follows from that finding, with a price you approve first. Keep any footage the plumber records.

Why A Camera Inspection Comes Before Any Repair

A waterproof camera on a flexible cable shows what the pipe actually looks like: roots, a cracked joint, an offset, a belly where the pipe has sagged, or a collapse. It also measures how far from the cleanout the problem sits, so a spot repair can be targeted instead of guessed at.

Camera reports are often graded with the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program defect codes, and it is fair to ask whether yours are. The footage is your record, so ask to see it. If one plumber says the line needs replacement and the video shows a single cracked joint, a repair may be all you need.

Older Pipe

What Is Under Older High Point Streets

Clay tile laterals with short sections and many joints are common under houses built before the 1960s, and each joint invites root growth. Mature trees along the tree-lined streets of Emerywood and Johnson Place seek moisture and find it at those joints. Some mid-century homes were built with bituminized fiber pipe, sometimes called Orangeburg, which softens and deforms with age.

Ranches built from the 1940s through the 1980s often have cast iron under the slab, which rusts and scales from the inside. Mill houses near Highland Cotton Mills may have lines that were patched many times. Each material fails differently, so the repair has to match. Where drains simply clog and recur, professional hydro jetting may be enough.

Emergency Steps

What To Do If Sewage Backs Up

Raw sewage carries bacteria and should be handled carefully.

  1. 1

    Stop using water everywhere in the house, including toilets, sinks, tubs and the washer.

  2. 2

    Keep children and pets away from the affected area and open nearby windows if the smell is strong.

  3. 3

    Do not use chemical drain openers, which can splash and harm the plumber.

  4. 4

    Photograph the damage before cleanup for insurance.

  5. 5

    Wear gloves and boots if you must enter the area, and wash thoroughly afterward.

  6. 6

    Call the 24/7 line and describe which fixtures are affected.

Permits And Digging

Before Anyone Digs

North Carolina law calls for contacting NC811 before excavation so buried utilities can be marked. The plumber normally handles that call, but confirm it is scheduled. Sewer excavation can also trigger permit and inspection requirements, which vary by jurisdiction, so ask the plumber to confirm them with the local inspections office.

Paperwork

Keep The Camera Footage And Ask For A Written Scope

Ask for the recording and for each defect marked by its distance from the cleanout. The file helps with insurance questions, permit applications and second opinions, and it shows a later plumber exactly where work was done.

After the repair the plumber should run water through the line, often with a second camera pass, to confirm the slope and the joints. Homeowner policies often exclude sewer damage from wear or roots, and some offer optional line coverage, so read the policy before repairs begin.

Roots And Partial Cracks

A Cracked Line With Roots: Spot Repair Or Bigger Job

Callers often describe a localized crack in a PVC or clay line with root intrusion, and they want a spot repair. That is a fair question. A camera shows the length of the damage, the number of defects and whether the rest of the pipe is sound.

If the line has one defect, a spot repair may be enough. If the camera shows several cracks, offsets or a belly, a longer repair or replacement is more honest. Ask for the footage and the distance of each defect from the cleanout.
