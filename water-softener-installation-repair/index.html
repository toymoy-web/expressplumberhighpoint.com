Do You Need One

High Point's City Water Is Already Soft

Hardness is measured in milligrams per liter, or in grains per gallon, and one grain per gallon is about 17 mg/L. The City of High Point's annual water quality reports, published from 2016 through 2019, listed hardness at roughly 30 to 60 mg/L, or about 2 to 3.5 grains. Water under about 60 mg/L is classed as soft.

That means a salt-based softener on city water usually has little to soften. A softener may still be justified for specific reasons, but it is rarely needed to protect the water heater or fixtures. A plumber should test your water and tell you so before selling one.

Repair Signals

Signs A Water Softener Needs Service

Hard-water spots return

The resin is exhausted or the softener is bypassed.

Salt level never drops

A salt bridge, a hard crust that blocks salt from dissolving.

Water won't regenerate

A failed control valve or timer.

Salty taste

A brine line or valve problem.

Loss of pressure

Resin beads or debris plugging the system.

Constant drain noise

A stuck valve sending brine to the drain.

Common Repairs

What Fails On A Softener, And How It Is Fixed

Salt bridges are the most common problem. The salt forms a crust over empty space, so no brine is made. Breaking the bridge and cleaning the tank usually restores it. Control valves, injectors and brine valves also wear, and resin beads last roughly 10 to 15 years before they need replacing.

Softeners are certified to NSF/ANSI Standard 44, which covers cation exchange units. When a unit is old and parts are scarce, replacement is usually more sensible than a rebuild. A plumber should say so plainly.

Alternatives

Softener Versus Filter

Salt-based softener

  • Removes hardness minerals by ion exchange
  • Needs salt and a drain connection
  • Does not remove chlorine or taste issues
  • Useful on hard well water

Whole-house filter

  • Reduces chlorine, sediment and some tastes
  • No salt, less maintenance
  • Does not soften hard water
  • A better fit for soft city water. See water filtration.
Septic Warning

Softener Backwash And Septic Tanks

Guilford County Environmental Health advises septic owners not to send water softener backwash into the septic tank. The salt-rich discharge and the volume of water can disturb the tank's biology and flood the drainfield. A softener on a septic property needs its backwash routed elsewhere, and that is a point for the plumber and the county to confirm.

That is one more reason to test before installing one. See septic tank service for how septic systems should be cared for.

Before Service

What To Do Before Servicing A Softener

Softeners are heavy, wet and connected to power and a drain.

  1. 1

    Put the softener in bypass so hard water flows to the house while it is being worked on.

  2. 2

    Unplug the control head before opening any part of the unit.

  3. 3

    Wear gloves and lift salt bags with your legs, since each bag is heavy.

  4. 4

    Mop up spilled brine promptly, because it corrodes concrete and metal.

  5. 5

    Note how long since the last service and what the settings are.

Local Water

Softeners And High Point's Water Supply

High Point's drinking water comes from Oak Hollow Lake, Arnold Koonce City Lake and the Ward Water Plant, and the city publishes an annual water quality report. Many homes on that supply do not need a softener, and an honest check of your own hardness is the right first step. The report lists hardness and mineral levels for your supply.

Houses on private wells near Trinity, Randleman and Thomasville may see harder water and iron staining. A softener also adds salt to wastewater, which matters for septic systems and should be discussed before installation.
