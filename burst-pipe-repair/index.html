Why Speed Matters

A Burst Pipe Moves Water Fast

Insurance guidance often cites a crack as small as one-eighth of an inch releasing roughly 250 gallons a day, and a full split in a supply line can release far more. Water runs along framing and soaks insulation before it shows at a ceiling or a baseboard. Closing the main valve is the single step that limits all of it.

Once the water is off, the repair is usually a matter of cutting out the failed section and replacing it. The harder questions are why it failed and whether the pipes around it are headed for the same fate. A good plumber answers both before leaving.

First Minutes

What To Do When A Pipe Bursts

These steps work for nearly every burst pipe.

  1. 1

    Close the main water valve. It is usually near the water heater, in the crawlspace or garage, or in a meter box near the curb.

  2. 2

    If you cannot find the main valve, close the shutoff in the meter box with a meter key or pliers, and tell the dispatcher.

  3. 3

    Turn off the water heater so it does not run dry, and switch off power only if the panel is dry and reachable.

  4. 4

    Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines and relieve pressure.

  5. 5

    Move furniture and valuables out of the wet area, and photograph the damage before cleanup.

  6. 6

    Call the 24/7 line and describe where the water is coming from.

Early Warnings

Signs A Pipe Is Failing Or Has Already Failed

Sound of running water

A hiss or trickle with every fixture closed points to a leak in the walls or slab.

Sudden low pressure

A large break upstream can drop pressure at every tap.

Stains or bulging paint

Brown rings on a ceiling or bubbling paint mark where water has traveled.

Warm floor spots

A hot-water line leaking under a slab can warm the floor above it.

Brown or rusty water

Corroding galvanized pipe sheds rust before it fails.

Frost on a pipe

Ice on an exposed line means the water inside may be freezing.

Winter Risk

Freeze Breaks In A Mild-Winter City

High Point winters are mild on average, which is exactly why pipes here get less protection than in colder states. A hard freeze that holds for several hours can split a copper or PEX line in an unvented crawlspace or an exterior wall. Mill houses with shallow crawlspaces are especially exposed.

Before a freeze, cover exposed pipes with foam sleeves, close foundation vents, and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. If a pipe does freeze, thaw it slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use an open flame, and never leave a frozen pipe unattended once water starts to flow.

After The Repair

Dealing With The Water That Is Already In The House

Repairing the pipe does not dry the structure. Drywall, insulation and flooring that stayed wet for a day or more need extraction and drying, which is the job of professional water damage restoration. If the same house has repeated failures, whole-home pipe replacement is worth pricing against a string of repairs.

A break in the line between the meter and the house is a different repair, covered under water line repair and replacement. Burst pipe calls come in from Trinity and the rest of southern Randolph County as well as High Point.

After The Repair

Drying And Documenting The Damage

Once the water is off and the pipe is fixed, drying matters. Wet drywall and carpet can grow mold in the days that follow, so open cabinet doors, run fans and remove standing water. If more than a small area is wet, water damage restoration is a separate service with its own dispatch.

Photograph the pipe, the water and the damaged items before cleanup. Keep the plumber's invoice and any removed section of pipe for the insurance claim.
