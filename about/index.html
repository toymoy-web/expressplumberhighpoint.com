What This Is

An Emergency Plumbing Line For High Point And Nearby Towns

High Point Express Plumbing is a website and phone line for people in High Point, North Carolina who need a plumber. Calls to the number on this site are answered 24 hours a day and routed to independent local plumbing contractors, who set their own prices and availability. This site is an advertising and call-routing service, and it says so in the footer of every page.

That arrangement matters for how you read the rest of the site. The pages explain problems, options and costs in plain language so you can make a decision. The contractor who takes your call does the work and stands behind it.

Plain Terms

What This Site Is And Is Not

This site is

  • A 24/7 phone line that connects callers with local plumbing contractors
  • A library of sourced, plain-language plumbing guides for the High Point area
  • A way to get a price quoted before emergency work begins
  • Free for non-emergency estimates

This site is not

  • A substitute for 911 or the gas utility in a gas leak or fire
  • A source of legal, insurance or medical advice
  • A review site with ratings, which it does not publish
  • A way to see one named technician or owner
How We Write

Where The Information On These Pages Comes From

Each page draws on public sources and says so. Water details come from the City of High Point's annual water quality reports. Septic guidance cites the state public health department and Guilford County Environmental Health. Leak figures come from the EPA's WaterSense program, and code references name the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the NC Plumbing Code based on it, which are the standards plumbers in the state follow.

Local history comes from town and state sources, such as the Town of Jamestown, the City of Trinity and the National Register districts in High Point. Where a fact varies by address, the page tells you to confirm it with the right office, such as City of High Point Public Services or your county environmental health department.

What You Will Not Find

No Invented Reviews, Ratings Or Credentials

You will not find star ratings, customer testimonials, years-in-business claims, named technicians or license numbers on this site. Those would be easy to invent and impossible for a reader to check, so they are left out. If the business behind this line adds a verifiable credential, it will be shown with proof.

That is a deliberate trade-off. It makes the pages plainer, but everything on them can be checked against a source. When you want proof of a plumber's license or insurance, ask for it directly. The next section shows how.

Protect Yourself

What To Do Before You Hire Any Plumber

These steps work for any contractor, whether you found them here or somewhere else.

  1. 1

    Ask for the plumber's North Carolina license number and verify it with the NC State Board of Examiners of Plumbing, Heating and Fire Sprinkler Contractors. A Class I plumbing license covers work in any structure, while a Class II license is limited to single-family detached homes.

  2. 2

    Ask for proof of liability insurance and, for larger jobs, workers' compensation coverage.

  3. 3

    Get the price in writing before work begins, and make sure it names the scope of work.

  4. 4

    Ask whether the job needs a permit and who will pull it. Water heater replacements, repipes and gas work commonly do.

  5. 5

    For septic pumping, ask for the contractor's septage management permit and where the waste is taken.

  6. 6

    Avoid paying the full amount in advance, and keep invoices and photos for your records.

Pricing

How Pricing Works On This Line

For emergencies, the plumber diagnoses the problem and quotes the repair before starting, and work begins after you approve the number. Estimates are free for non-emergency work such as water heater replacement, repiping, fixture installation and filtration. Prices are not posted on the site because they depend on the house, the access and the part.

Each service page lists the factors that move the price, so you know what to ask about. If a quote seems far off, it is reasonable to ask the plumber to explain each line, or to get a second opinion before approving work that is not urgent.

Limits

What The Line Does Not Cover

Some jobs fall outside a plumbing line. Fire sprinkler systems and medical gas piping are separate trades, well pumps and pressure tanks are separate equipment, and mold testing and health questions belong with other professionals. The relevant pages say so where it matters.

Water damage restoration and water mitigation are separate services with a dedicated phone line, shown on those two pages. Plumbing repairs, including burst pipe repair, use the main plumbing number. See restoration after water damage if you need drying and cleanup after a leak.
