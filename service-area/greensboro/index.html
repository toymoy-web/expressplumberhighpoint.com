Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In A Greensboro Home? Before We Arrive

Many west Greensboro houses and rental units are decades old, and the shutoff valve is not always where a newer house would have it.

1

Find and close the main valve

In many houses it is at the meter at the curb, on a front wall, or in the garage. Renters can ask the landlord or building manager for the valve location.

2

Switch off power to the wet zone

Shut off breakers for the affected rooms, including rooms below a leaking ceiling. Keep everyone out of standing water until the power is off.

3

Protect belongings and take photos

Move furniture and electronics out of the way and photograph the damage for the landlord or insurer before cleaning up.

Houses From The 1970s And 1980s

Emergency Plumbing In Homes Built Forty Or Fifty Years Ago

Most houses in ZIP 27410 and 27407 date to the 1970s and 1980s, and that era leaves a clear plumbing pattern. Original tank water heaters have mostly been replaced once or twice, and a replacement from the 2010s may be nearing the end now. Some houses from roughly 1978 to 1995 also have polybutylene supply lines, a gray plastic tubing that can fail at fittings without warning.

A pinhole leak in an old line is a good reason to ask for hidden leak detection before opening walls. If tubing is failing in several places, repiping may cost less over time than repeated repairs. A plumber can tell what you have from a quick look at the lines near the water heater.

Older Drains

Drain Lines, Roots And Backups

Mature trees are one of the best things about these neighborhoods and one of the hardest on old sewer laterals. Roots enter at joints in clay or cast-iron pipe and slowly choke the line, and the first sign is usually a drain that clears and then slows again.

A slow main line that keeps coming back is a case for hydro jetting after a camera inspection. A collapsed section calls for professional sewer line repair instead. The camera tells you which one before anyone digs.

What To Do In A Plumbing Emergency In Your Greensboro Home

A few quick actions can save a ceiling or a floor.

  1. 1

    Locate the main valve at the curb meter, in the garage or by the heater, and close it fully.

  2. 2

    Switch off the water heater when the leak is close to it, and keep out of any pooled water near outlets.

  3. 3

    Stop flushing and running sinks if sewage is coming up, and keep children and pets clear of it.

  4. 4

    Take photos for the landlord or the insurer before anything gets cleaned up.

  5. 5

    Call the 24/7 line with the street address, the ZIP code and a description of what you see.
