Calling An Emergency Plumber In Trinity, NC 27370

Trinity is a Randolph County city of about 7,000 people in the northwest corner of the county, south of High Point. The 24/7 emergency plumber line serves ZIP 27370 and the surrounding rural roads, for burst pipes, sewer backups, water heater failures and septic emergencies.

The name comes from Trinity College, which started as Brown's Schoolhouse in 1838, took the name Trinity in 1859 and moved to Durham in 1892, where it became Duke University. The old college building is gone, though the Braxton Craven School keeps a gazebo with columns from its auditorium and the college chapel's bell. The town was incorporated in 1869.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock in Trinity, including for septic backups that start on a weekend night. The dispatcher asks early whether the home is on town sewer, a septic tank or a private well, because that answer decides who is sent.

Before We Arrive

Burst Pipe Or Active Leak In Trinity? Do This Before We Arrive

Many Trinity homes run on a well pump or a septic tank. The first steps differ a little from a house on city water.

1

Shut the water off

On city water, close the main valve at the meter. On a well, switch off the pump breaker so the pressure tank stops feeding the leak.

2

Turn off power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for wet rooms. If water is near the well pressure tank or the water heater, leave the power off until a plumber has checked it.

3

Ease up on a septic system

If drains are backing up, skip showers, laundry and the dishwasher. Extra water only pushes more wastewater toward the house.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher your address, whether you have a well or a septic tank and what you see. That decides which truck and tools are sent.

Septic Homes

What Septic Owners In Trinity Should Know

About half of North Carolina homes use septic systems, according to the state's public health department. Most tanks need pumping every three to five years, and the pumper should check both sides of the tank. When every drain in the house slows at once, a full tank is a common cause.

Septic pumping covers the routine work. If the line between the house and the tank is damaged, emergency sewer line repair comes next. Keep softener backwash and grease out of the tank, and limit use of a garbage disposal, as county health guidance advises.

Older Homes

Homes Near The Old College Center Versus The Subdivisions

The town's oldest buildings date to the college era, so homes near the original center tend to be older than the subdivisions that grew up around them. Older houses may carry galvanized supply lines or cast-iron drains, while later homes use copper, PEX and PVC. A plumber sizes up the house's age on the first call.

Septic Backup

What To Do If Your Septic System Backs Up

Raw sewage carries bacteria, so limit contact until a professional has looked at it.

  1. 1

    Stop using water in the house, including toilets, sinks, showers and the washer.

  2. 2

    Keep children and pets away from surfacing sewage in the yard or inside the house.

  3. 3

    Never open or lean into the septic tank, because the gases inside can be dangerous.

  4. 4

    Check for a red alarm light on a pump tank panel and tell the dispatcher what you see.

  5. 5

    Call the 24/7 line and describe which fixtures are backing up.
