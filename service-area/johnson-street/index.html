About Johnson Street

Plumbing On Johnson Street, High Point

Johnson Street is a residential historic street in High Point. In 1987 it became the city's first local historic district, covering about 14 acres and 46 properties on both sides of the street. The district runs from East Parkway Avenue to just south of East Lexington Avenue.

The street also lies inside the larger Uptown Suburbs Historic District, which the National Register listed in 2013. Its original development, Johnson Place, was platted in 1907 at the east end of that district. The plans included paved roads, sidewalks, water and sewer systems and lighting.

Who Owns The Pipe

Public Mains And Your Own Line On A Planned Street

The 1907 plans for Johnson Place called for water and sewer systems, which is why a backup here raises a familiar question. Where does your line end and the public main begin? The answer changes who pays for a repair.

As general plumbing information, the owner usually maintains the line from the house to the connection and the utility maintains the main. Where that boundary sits varies, so confirm it with the City of High Point before assuming. Houses close to the street also mean a short run between the front door and the meter box, which helps when you tell a plumber where to start.

If A Drain Backs Up

Four Checks Before You Call About A Backup

  1. 1

    Stop adding water

    Do not run the dishwasher, washer or shower while a drain is slow or backing up. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere.

  2. 2

    Count the fixtures

    One clogged fixture points to a branch drain. Several fixtures backing up at once point to the main line.

  3. 3

    Look for the cleanout

    A capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard is the cleanout. Do not open it if sewage is standing in the house.

  4. 4

    Describe it on the call

    Say which fixtures are affected, how long it has been happening and whether anything unusual went down the drain.
