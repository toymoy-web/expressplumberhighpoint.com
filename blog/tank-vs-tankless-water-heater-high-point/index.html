Tank Vs Tankless Water Heater In High Point, NC

Neither type wins every time. The right pick depends on your gas line, your household and how you use hot water.

The Short Version

Tankless Costs More Up Front And Lasts Longer

A tankless water heater heats water only when you open a tap, so it never runs out and wastes no energy keeping a tank hot. A tank heater costs less up front and is simpler to install, but it can run out of hot water and loses some heat while it sits. Which wins depends on your house.

The U.S. Department of Energy says demand water heaters can be 24 to 34 percent more energy efficient than storage tanks in homes that use about 41 gallons of hot water a day or less. In homes that use a lot, the gain is 8 to 14 percent. The savings are real but smaller in busy households.

Side By Side

How The Two Types Compare

Tank water heater

  • Lower purchase and install cost
  • Works with existing gas line and vent in most homes
  • Limited hot water until it recovers
  • Typical life of 10 to 15 years

Tankless water heater

  • Higher purchase and install cost
  • Often needs a larger gas line and new venting
  • Continuous hot water, within the unit's flow limit
  • Typical life of 20 years or more

Gas And Venting

The Part Most Buyers Miss: Gas Line And Venting

A tankless gas unit can draw close to 200,000 BTU per hour, several times what a tank uses. Many 1940s to 1980s houses in the High Point area have gas piping sized for a stove and a furnace, so the line may need upsizing. Venting is also different, often through a wall with special pipe.

Those extras explain most of the price gap between the two types. Ask any plumber to measure the existing gas line and meter before quoting. The page on gas line service explains how gas lines are sized.

Soft Water

Does High Point's Soft Water Change The Answer?

Tankless advice is usually written for hard-water cities where scale threatens the heat exchanger. The City of High Point's published hardness readings run roughly 30 to 60 mg/L, which is soft. Scale builds slowly here, so the maintenance penalty for tankless is milder than the national warnings suggest.

Annual flushing is still recommended by manufacturers, and it often matters for warranty. See tankless service for what a flush involves.

Rules

Federal Rules And Heat Pumps

Federal efficiency rules in place since April 2015 mean most electric tanks above 55 gallons are heat pump models. That makes the heat pump option relevant for all-electric homes replacing a large electric tank. A heat pump unit needs roughly 700 to 1,000 cubic feet of air space and a condensate drain, so it suits garages and basements better than closets.

Whatever you pick, work follows the NC Plumbing Code, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Permit rules depend on scope, and many like-for-like replacements are exempt under state law. The installer should confirm with the local inspections office.

Real Costs

What The Price Difference Is Made Of

The gap between tank and tankless comes from the parts around the unit, not only the unit itself. A tankless gas heater may need a larger gas line, new sealed venting, isolation valves for flushing, a condensate drain on condensing models and an electrical outlet for its controls. Each is a line item on the quote.

A good estimate lists all of them. If two quotes differ widely, compare what each includes. Skipping isolation valves, for example, makes the unit cheaper now and harder to flush later.

Sizing

Hot Water Habits Decide The Size

Tankless units are sized by flow rate and temperature rise. A shower and a bathroom faucet together may draw four or five gallons a minute, and a dishwasher or washer adds more. Incoming water is colder in winter, so the same unit delivers less hot flow in January than in July. Ask the plumber to size for your coldest month.

Tank heaters are sized by first-hour rating, the hot water the tank can supply in a busy hour. Households with a large soaking tub or several morning showers should size up. Either way, describe your peak hour to the plumber instead of guessing.

Permits In High Point

Switching Types Can Change The Permit And The Gas Line

Moving from a tank to a tankless unit often changes the gas piping, the vent and sometimes the electrical supply. In High Point, the Development Services Division of the city's Planning and Development department handles permits at 211 South Hamilton Street. Its permit guide says that when a permit is required it must be issued before work begins.

Ask your plumber whether your swap needs a permit and who pulls it. The guide lists (336) 883-3151, permits@highpointnc.gov and buildhighpoint.com for questions. Homeowners in Trinity and Thomasville should ask their local permit office, since each town handles its own process, and see tankless water heater service for flushing and repairs.
