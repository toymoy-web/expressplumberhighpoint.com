Why Is My High Point Water Bill So High?

A jump in your water bill with no change in habits usually means water is escaping. Two quick tests can confirm it.

Run The Meter Test Before Anything Else

The fastest way to tell whether a leak exists is to read the water meter with everything off. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker and irrigation timer. Then look at the meter's leak indicator, a small dial or triangle that spins when water moves.

If it spins, or the digits change over 30 minutes, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. If it stays still, the high bill probably comes from use, a billing issue or a leak that only happens when a fixture runs, such as a toilet or irrigation valve.

Test Fixtures

What To Do To Test For Leaks Yourself

You can rule out the most common sources in about half an hour.

  1. 1

    Add a few drops of food coloring to each toilet tank and wait 10 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a leaking flapper.

  2. 2

    Check under every sink for dampness at the supply lines and traps.

  3. 3

    Look at the water heater's base and relief valve discharge pipe for moisture.

  4. 4

    Walk the yard after a dry spell and look for soggy or unusually green patches along the line to the house.

  5. 5

    Run the meter test again after fixing anything, to see if the movement stopped.

Where Leaks Hide

Hidden Leaks In High Point Houses

Many south-side ranches built from the 1940s through the 1980s have supply pipes in or under the concrete slab. A leak there may show only as a warm patch on the floor, a hiss in a quiet house or a bill that creeps up. Mill houses and older homes often have crawlspaces where drips run unseen for years.

Older Uptown Suburbs houses may have galvanized supply lines that pinhole at the joints. Houses built between roughly 1978 and 1995 might have polybutylene tubing. Each can leak inside walls, and each is a job for professional leak detection before anyone cuts drywall.

Outside The House

The Line Between The Meter And The House

If the meter moves with the main shutoff at the house closed, the leak is outside, in the buried service line. Customers generally own that pipe, so a leak is their repair. Confirm the boundary with City of High Point Public Services if you are unsure.

Slab leaks are a separate repair, covered under slab leak service. For a leaking toilet, toilet repair usually fixes it with a flapper or fill valve.

The Bill

Reading The Bill Before You Hunt For A Leak

Start with the bill itself. Check the length of the billing period, whether the reading was actual or estimated, and how this month compares with the same month last year. An estimated reading can be corrected by the next real one, and a longer billing period naturally shows more use.

Sewer charges are often based on water use, so a leak in the water supply can raise both lines. If a reading looks wrong, ask the utility to reread the meter. Keep the bills, since a repaired leak sometimes qualifies for an adjustment.

Not Always A Leak

Everyday Causes Of A Higher Bill

Not every jump is a leak. Lawn irrigation, filling a pool, houseguests, a new baby, a humidifier and a stretch of hot weather all raise use. A new appliance or a changed laundry routine can add more than people expect. These show on the meter only when the fixtures run.

The meter test separates the two. If the dial is still with everything off, the cause is use. If it moves, a leak exists, and a running toilet is the most common hidden source.

Fixing The Usual Suspects

Cheap Fixes For The Most Common Leaks

Most household leaks are small parts. A toilet flapper, a fill valve, a faucet cartridge, a worn supply line and a leaking showerhead are inexpensive to replace, and fixing them can lower the bill. The EPA estimates that fixing easily corrected leaks can save about ten percent on a water bill.

If you replace a part and the meter still moves, the leak is elsewhere. That is the point to call a plumber, since in-wall, under-slab and buried leaks need tools you do not have.

Next Steps

What To Do With The Results

If the meter test is clean and the toilets pass the dye test, your bill probably reflects use. Look at habits, irrigation and guests. If the meter moves, write down what you found and call a plumber before the leak gets worse.

Keep a simple log of meter readings, bills and repairs. It helps the plumber, supports any adjustment request and shows you quickly if the problem returns.
