Frozen Pipes In The Piedmont: How To Prevent And Thaw Them

Mild winters make people careless, but one hard freeze can split a pipe in an unprotected crawlspace or exterior wall.

Why It Happens Here

Mild Winters Leave Pipes Unprotected

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the NC Plumbing Code follows, says pipes in exterior walls, attics and crawlspaces must be protected from freezing by insulation, heat or both. Water expands as it freezes, and that pressure is what splits a pipe, not the ice itself.

High Point winters are mild on average, so many homes were built with pipes in unheated crawlspaces, garages and exterior walls with little insulation. When a hard freeze arrives, those pipes are the first to go.

The most useful step is to know where your exposed pipes are and protect them before the forecast turns cold. This guide covers prevention, what to do if a pipe freezes and when to call a plumber.

Before The Freeze

What To Do Before A Hard Freeze

A few steps cost little and prevent the most damage.

  1. 1

    Disconnect garden hoses and drain or cover outdoor spigots.

  2. 2

    Close foundation vents and seal obvious gaps around crawlspace pipes.

  3. 3

    Open sink cabinet doors against exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes.

  4. 4

    Let a faucet drip, a thin steady stream, on the coldest nights, choosing one that is fed by pipes in an exposed wall or crawlspace.

  5. 5

    Keep the thermostat at a steady setting, even if you are away.

  6. 6

    Locate the main shutoff valve so you can close it quickly if a pipe splits.

If It Freezes

How To Thaw A Frozen Pipe Safely

  1. 1

    Open the faucet

    Let water flow as the pipe thaws.

  2. 2

    Warm the pipe

    Use a hair dryer or warm towels, starting nearest the faucet.

  3. 3

    Never use flame

    No torch, heat gun or open flame, which can start fires or damage pipe.

  4. 4

    Check for splits

    Once water flows, look for leaks along the pipe.

Local Context

Mill Houses, Crawlspaces And Vacant Units

Mill houses near Highland Cotton Mills often sit over shallow crawlspaces with exposed lines, and older Uptown Suburbs houses may have additions with poorly insulated pipes. Landlords with vacant rentals face the biggest risk, because a split pipe in an empty unit can run for days. Check unoccupied properties after the first hard freeze.

If you find a split, close the main valve and see emergency burst pipe repair. A house that has frozen more than once might need rerouted pipe, and whole-home repiping can move vulnerable lines inside the heated space.

After The Thaw

Check For Hidden Damage

A pipe can crack while frozen and leak only when it thaws and water pressure returns. Check ceilings, crawlspace floors and the meter after any freeze. If the meter keeps moving with everything off, see the leak detection service page for next steps. Residents of Trinity and other rural parts of the area, where outbuildings and well houses also freeze, should check those too.

Whole-House Prep

Beyond The Obvious Pipes

Think about every place water sits in the cold. A water heater in an unheated garage, a washing machine supply line on an exterior wall, an irrigation system with a backflow preventer above ground, a pool or outdoor shower line and a hose bib that serves the garden all deserve a look. Draining or insulating them before a freeze costs little.

A rough sketch of where supply lines run through the crawlspace and exterior walls helps. When a freeze is forecast, you know exactly where to open a cabinet, add a sleeve or let a faucet drip.

After A Freeze

Documentation And Insurance

If a pipe splits, take photos and video before cleanup, keep receipts and tell your insurer. Policies commonly expect you to take reasonable steps to stop further damage, such as closing the main and removing standing water. Drying wet structure quickly limits mold, and water damage restoration covers that work.

Save the failed section of pipe if you can. It helps the plumber and the adjuster see why it failed. Many policies cover a sudden freeze burst and the water damage it causes, but not the cost of the pipe itself.

Away From Home

Vacation Homes, Rentals And Long Trips

An empty house is the most at risk. If you will be gone through a cold stretch, keep the heat on at a steady setting, ask someone to check the house daily and know where the main shutoff is. For longer absences, close the main valve and drain the lines, which a plumber can do.

Rental owners should check vacant units after the first hard freeze and leave a note with the shutoff location. A frozen line in an empty unit can run for days. A water alarm near the water heater and under sinks helps catch problems sooner.

Next Steps

A Short Pre-Winter Routine

Each fall, walk the house with a flashlight. Note any pipe in a crawlspace, garage, attic or exterior wall, and check the insulation. Disconnect hoses, test the main shutoff and replace any cracked foam sleeves. Make sure the crawlspace vents close.

Add a reminder to your calendar for the first cold forecast. Ten minutes of preparation beats an afternoon of cleanup. If you find a pipe that has frozen before, mention it to a plumber, since rerouting it can end the problem for good.
