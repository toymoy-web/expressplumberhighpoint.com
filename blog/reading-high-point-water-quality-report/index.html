Reading The High Point Water Quality Report

The city's annual report is long and technical. Here is what each part tells you and how it affects your plumbing.

What The Report Is

An Annual Report On The Water That Reaches Your Tap

Public water systems publish an annual drinking water quality report, also called a consumer confidence report. The City of High Point's report is issued under public water system ID NC0241020 and describes where the water comes from, how it is treated and what testing found. It is the best single source for what is in your tap water.

Most people never read it. The sections below explain what each part means and what is useful for a homeowner, without making health claims. For any health concern, the report and a physician are the right authorities.

Sources

Where High Point's Water Comes From

The city's water comes from a 62-square-mile watershed in the headwaters of the Cape Fear River system, collected in Oak Hollow Lake and Arnold Koonce City Lake. Most of it is pumped from City Lake and treated at the Ward Water Plant. The city also receives treated water from the Piedmont Triad Regional Water Authority.

The report's source water assessment rated both lakes as higher susceptibility in September 2020. The report notes that this rating describes potential for contamination, not the quality of water delivered to homes.

Hardness

The Number That Matters Most For Plumbing

Hardness readings in the city's annual reports from 2016 through 2019 ran roughly 30 to 60 mg/L. One grain per gallon is about 17 mg/L, so that is roughly 2 to 3.5 grains, which counts as soft. Soft water leaves little scale on heaters and fixtures.

That is why a salt-based softener on city water is rarely needed. See water softener installation and repair for when one is justified, such as on a private well.

Using The Report

What To Do With What You Read

A few practical steps turn the report into decisions.

  1. 1

    Find the latest report on the City of High Point's website, and note the year.

  2. 2

    Look at the hardness figure to decide whether a softener makes sense.

  3. 3

    Read the treatment section if chlorine taste bothers you.

  4. 4

    Check whether any substance was detected near its limit.

  5. 5

    Remember the report covers water at the plant and in the system, not your house plumbing.

At Home

Why The Water At Your Tap Can Differ From The Report

The report describes water as it leaves the system. Older houses can add lead from old solder or fixtures, rust from galvanized lines and metals from corroding pipe. Houses built before the 1986 lead ban on solder and brass fixtures are the usual concern, which covers much of the Uptown Suburbs and mill-village housing.

If you want to reduce taste or specific contaminants, see water filtration installation. Look for filters certified to NSF/ANSI standards for what you want to reduce. If old pipes are the source, a full repipe project removes it.

Nearby

Other Utilities In The Area

Not every address uses High Point water. Jamestown runs its own municipal system, and rural properties use wells. Read the report for your own utility, and test a private well.

Reading The Tables

Units, Limits And What They Mean

Test tables use milligrams per liter, equal to parts per million, and micrograms per liter, equal to parts per billion. A maximum contaminant level is the legal limit for a substance, and an action level is a threshold that triggers treatment steps. Some results are annual averages and others are single readings.

Finding a substance in a table does not mean the limit was exceeded. Compare the detected level with the limit in the same row. If you are unsure how to interpret a result, the water utility's contact in the report can explain it.

Wells

Private Wells Do Not Appear In The Report

The city report covers the city's system. A private well is the owner's responsibility, and its quality is only known by testing. Local health departments can explain how to collect a sample and which tests are worth doing, including bacteria and, in some areas, other contaminants.

Test a well when you buy a house, after flooding or repairs, and on a regular schedule. Keep the lab results, since they matter when you sell.

Ask

Questions To Ask The Water Utility

If something in the report is unclear, ask the utility directly. Good questions include which source supplies your neighborhood, whether the water is blended, what disinfectant is used and how hardness is measured. Ask whether any construction or main flushing is planned near you, since that can affect color for a day or two.

You can also ask about service line materials, since federal rules required utilities to inventory them by October 2024. Knowing your line's material is useful if you are weighing filtration or repiping.

Next Steps

Using The Report Well

Read the report once a year, when it is published. Note the hardness, the treatment steps and anything that changed. If you have questions, contact the utility using the details in the report.

Pair the report with what you can see and taste at home. If the water seems different from the report, check your own plumbing, filters and fixtures first, and then call the utility if the problem is not inside your house.

The 2025 Report

What The City's 2025 Edition Lists

The 2025 report for water system NC0241020 says the city's watershed covers 62 square miles, with streams forming the east and west forks of the Deep River. Water is stored in Oak Hollow Lake and Arnold Koonce City Lake, and most is pumped from Arnold Koonce City Lake and treated at the Ward Water Plant on Pendleton Street. In 2025 the city also bought water from the Piedmont Triad Regional Water Authority and Davidson Water Inc.

The report gives a Higher susceptibility rating for both lakes, based on a September 2020 assessment, and lists lead tap results from 2024 with none of 56 sites over the action level. It does not state a hardness figure. Questions go to Water Resources at (336) 883-3166. For taste or filter questions in Jamestown or Trinity, see water filtration installation.
